When a group parent guarantees a loan or facility for a subsidiary, an associate or a related party, it takes on a **corporate financial guarantee (CFG)**. It does not show up as debt on day one, but it is a significant **contingent liability**: if the borrower defaults, the guarantor pays.

## Why CFGs slip through the cracks

In many groups, CFGs are tracked in a spreadsheet kept by one person, or in the legal team's files. That makes it hard to answer basic questions quickly:

- How much has the group guaranteed in total, and for whom?
- Which guarantees expire or need renewal soon?
- Which bank facilities depend on which guarantees?
